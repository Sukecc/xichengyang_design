The Unified Voice across Scripts.

Lyean (Lián 连), which translates to ‘connection’ in Chinese, is a screen-reading typeface family containing three scripts, Latin, Chinese simplified Hanzi and Arabic. The aim of this typeface is to build a connection between each Chinese ethnic minority script, exchanging cultures and building a comfortable, warm cultural space.

Lyean brings a new atmosphere to screen reading, with slightly crunchy serif, organic shapes. From optical to display to sans, Lyean can adapt many typographic genres including small text on smart watches or outstanding titles on website pages.

This is a project for the typeface design master’s course in the University of Reading, but it is still on-going and involves the expansion of Hangeul, Japanese Kana and Greek.

Biography

A practice centred
on typographic voice.

I am a typeface designer and researcher currently based in the UK. I graduated with distinction from the MA in Typeface Design at the University of Reading (2022), where I subsequently completed my doctorate in 2026. My research focuses on the development of digital typefaces for the Tangut script—an extinct writing system used in northwestern China between the 11th and 13th centuries.

Throughout my practice, I have worked on various multi-script projects involving Latin, Chinese, and Japanese Kanji. My independent font design has won awards, including the New York TDC Award and recognition from the Tokyo TDC for AraTangut. My research has been presented globally at ATypI Stanford (2026), ATypI (2021), and the Chinese Embassy in the UK.
