Tangut Character Composition and Search Tool

I built this interactive search tool to solve a very specific pain point in my type design workflow: finding and reusing Tangut components quickly.

Tangut characters are incredibly complex. When designing a new typeface, trying to find a reusable radical from thousands of unindexed characters feels like looking for a needle in a haystack. Often, even if you find the right component, it is in the wrong size or scale, forcing you to look all over again. This tool allows me to select specific strokes or stroke orders to instantly filter out every single character that contains that exact radical. This narrows down the ocean of data to a few clean results, making it effortless to find a perfectly proportioned component to reuse or adjust.

To speed up the project, I also added a progress highlighter. You can import your font production logs to highlight the characters that are already designed and ready to be recycled. Furthermore, this system allows scholars and designers to find characters visually by their shape and strokes, without needing to know how the radical is pronounced.

Dataset & Component Access

The underlying database contains every Unicode-encoded Tangut component, completely broken down by stroke order and individual sub-parts. This allows users to search flexibly, even if they do not input the exact sequential stroke order.

The video demonstration above shows how the application works in real time. To protect the dataset from automated harvesting, the full source code matrix is kept private. If you are interested in accessing the comprehensive stroke database for research, please contact: threecczhu@gmail.com.

Data Provenance

This platform integrates baseline cross-referencing records from the BabelSoft Script Data: Tangut Merged Character Data, structurally enhanced and expanded with original stroke sequence mapping independently generated by Xicheng Yang. All rights reserved.

Biography

A practice centred
on typographic voice.

I am a typeface designer and researcher currently based in the UK. I graduated with distinction from the MA in Typeface Design at the University of Reading (2022), where I subsequently completed my doctorate in 2026. My research focuses on the development of digital typefaces for the Tangut script—an extinct writing system used in northwestern China between the 11th and 13th centuries.

Throughout my practice, I have worked on various multi-script projects involving Latin, Chinese, and Japanese Kanji. My independent font design has won awards, including the New York TDC Award and recognition from the Tokyo TDC for AraTangut. My research has been presented globally at ATypI Stanford (2026), ATypI (2021), and the Chinese Embassy in the UK.
