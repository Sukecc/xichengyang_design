Linguistic & Musical Context

Guqin Notation, historically known as Jianzipu (减字谱), is a unique ideographic music notation system developed in China during the Tang Dynasty. Unlike Western staff notation which records pitch, Jianzipu acts as a structural formula—combining Chinese character strokes into single compound clusters to document precise finger positions, left-hand slide techniques, and right-hand plucking methods. Preserving this heritage in the digital age presents a monumental typographic bottleneck due to the near-infinite variable combinations of stroke symbols.

Digital Infrastructure

Traditional Chinese Music Notation Digital Infrastructure.

The project is a precedent for typeface design without codepoints, counting over 200,000 reduced character forms, following ancient norms for strokes and structure, and designed in a unified standard. It is also the only character database project development for the international encoding of the notation system for Guqin and will be used in the future.

Biography

A practice centred
on typographic voice.

I am a typeface designer and researcher currently based in the UK. I graduated with distinction from the MA in Typeface Design at the University of Reading (2022), where I subsequently completed my doctorate in 2026. My research focuses on the development of digital typefaces for the Tangut script—an extinct writing system used in northwestern China between the 11th and 13th centuries.

Throughout my practice, I have worked on various multi-script projects involving Latin, Chinese, and Japanese Kanji. My independent font design has won awards, including the New York TDC Award and recognition from the Tokyo TDC for AraTangut. My research has been presented globally at ATypI Stanford (2026), ATypI (2021), and the Chinese Embassy in the UK.
